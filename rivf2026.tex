\begin{abstract}
Modern multi-campus higher education infrastructures face escalating cyber threats driven by high-density IoT deployments, unmanaged student endpoints, and massive distributed telemetry flows. Centralized security architectures suffer from wide-area bandwidth saturation, student data privacy vulnerabilities, and extended containment latency. In this paper, we propose a privacy-aware, event-driven \textbf{Federated Edge-Cloud Resilience Architecture} engineered specifically for distributed campus environments. The platform deploys lightweight PyTorch dynamically INT8-quantized Temporal Convolutional Network and Gated Recurrent Unit (TCN-GRU) sequence detectors directly at edge gateways, executing localized anomaly scoring and participating in collaborative Federated Averaging (FedAvg) aggregation without raw telemetry egress. Enriched high-confidence security events are published over an asynchronous Redis Streams bus to a cloud-based dynamic risk engine and a Deep Q-Network (DQN) Software-Defined Networking (SDN) controller for automated closed-loop containment. Evaluated across $K=5$ federated campus clients on the InSDN benchmark with additional cross-dataset zero-shot evaluation on CSE-CIC-IDS2018, the proposed framework achieves a binary anomaly detection F1-score of $0.8582$ ($\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9729$, $\text{PR-AUC} = 0.9567$) under uniform partitioning, outperforming isolated uncoordinated local baselines ($0.8314$). Under non-IID Dirichlet-skewed ($\alpha=0.5$) client attack distributions, the global model achieves $F1 = 0.8097$, demonstrating stable collaborative convergence despite statistical heterogeneity. The edge compute pipeline completes in $8.290 \pm 0.140$~ms (with single-sequence INT8 scoring in $4.555$~ms), and automated closed-loop OpenFlow mitigation completes in $12.34 \pm 0.55$~ms on Open vSwitch datapaths. Evaluated over $100$ test episodes across 5 random seeds ($500$ total evaluation episodes), the learned DQN policy achieves a $98.4 \pm 0.6\%$ threat containment rate with only $2.1 \pm 0.4\%$ false quarantine, substantially outperforming static threshold heuristics ($14.8 \pm 1.2\%$ false isolation). Furthermore, dynamic INT8 quantization compresses the GRU and linear model footprint to $0.027$~MB ($27.5$~KB), maintaining minimal edge CPU utilization ($13.8\%$) under sustained throughput of $10{,}000$~events/sec.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Higher education institutions operate geographically distributed campus networks connecting academic departments, student residential dormitories, open research laboratories, and centralized administrative databases. The proliferation of unmanaged Bring-Your-Own-Device (BYOD) endpoints and high-density Internet of Things (IoT) sensors (e.g., smart surveillance, building automation, environmental monitoring) significantly expands the perimeter attack surface. Traditional enterprise defense paradigms rely on centralized Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) platforms and inline Deep Packet Inspection (DPI) proxies. However, this centralized approach exhibits critical architectural limitations in modern multi-campus environments:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Privacy Concerns and WAN Bandwidth Saturation}: Continuous backhauling of raw packet traces or granular NetFlow streams across Wide Area Network (WAN) links saturates satellite inter-campus uplinks and risks violating regional student data privacy mandates.
    \item \textbf{High Response Latency and Alert Fatigue}: Centralized manual triage workflows in Security Operations Centers (SOC) can experience substantial response delays and triage bottlenecks during aggressive volumetric or lateral threat propagation (e.g., ransomware, ARP poisoning, credential brute-forcing).
\end{enumerate}

To address these challenges, we present a system-level co-design of \textbf{Federated Edge-Cloud Resilience Architecture} tailored for distributed campus networks. Our system decouples threat detection and mitigation into a coordinated 4-tier event-driven hierarchy:
(i) localized edge telemetry extraction with PyTorch dynamically INT8-quantized sequence detectors;
(ii) privacy-aware Federated Averaging (FedAvg) model parameter coordination;
(iii) asynchronous Redis Streams event routing coupled with spatial-temporal identity-context fusion; and
(iv) closed-loop Deep Q-Network (DQN) Software-Defined Networking (SDN) policy enforcement.

The key contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Quantized Edge Sequence Detection}: We design a hybrid Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) detector with dynamic INT8 quantization on recurrent and linear layers ($0.027$~MB footprint), executing sub-$5$~ms unbatched flow scoring at distributed campus edge taps.
    \item \textbf{Privacy-Aware Collaborative Training}: We implement a FedAvg aggregation protocol across distributed campus clients, demonstrating collaborative training stability under uniform client partitioning ($F1 = 0.8582$ vs. $0.8314$ uncoordinated local baseline) and characterized non-IID Dirichlet evaluation distributions ($F1 = 0.8097$).
    \item \textbf{Context-Enriched Event Pipeline}: We construct an asynchronous event pipeline over Redis Streams incorporating dynamic identity-to-role risk scoring to eliminate transient false alarms.
    \item \textbf{Learned Closed-Loop DQN SDN Containment}: We formulate an adaptive Deep Q-Network (DQN) SDN controller with discrete action spaces (OpenFlow rate-limiting, VLAN quarantine, flow drop) achieving an automated closed-loop mitigation latency of $12.34 \pm 0.55$~ms with minimal benign user disruption.
    \item \textbf{Empirical System Benchmark}: We present detailed experimental evaluations on InSDN and CSE-CIC-IDS2018 benchmarks, validating per-stage latency, FedAvg convergence, DQN policy trade-offs, edge CPU/RAM scaling up to $10{,}000$~events/sec, and an artifact package prepared for release upon acceptance.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work \& Novelty Positioning}
\subsection{Federated Learning in Network Intrusion Detection}
Federated Learning (FL), pioneered by McMahan et al.~\cite{b_fedavg}, enables collaborative model training across distributed clients without sharing raw private data. In network security, collaborative FL has been applied to IoT anomaly detection and DDoS mitigation~\cite{b_fl_survey, b_al_garadi}. Kianpisheh and Taleb~\cite{b_al_garadi} investigated collaborative FL paired with DRL-based control for 6G DDoS mitigation. However, several representative approaches evaluate unquantized 32-bit floating-point (FP32) recurrent models, which impose prohibitive memory and compute footprints on constrained campus edge gateways.

\subsection{Sequence Modeling and Edge Model Quantization}
Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN)~\cite{b_tcn} leverage dilated causal convolutions to capture extended historical receptive fields with lower computational complexity than standard Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN). Recent studies have explored hybrid convolutional and recurrent architectures (e.g., CNN/TCN and GRU) for sequential telemetry modeling in SDN environments~\cite{b_tcn_gru_iiot}. Nevertheless, deploying deep sequential detectors at edge gateways requires post-training dynamic quantization to minimize runtime latency and memory saturation. Our work customizes dynamic INT8 post-training quantization~\cite{b_pytorch_quant} for TCN-GRU flow detectors, compressing storage to $0.027$~MB while preserving detection fidelity.

\subsection{Automated SDN Mitigation and DRL Control}
Software-Defined Networking (SDN) provides programmable centralized control of network forwarding planes via OpenFlow protocols~\cite{b_sdn_survey, b_openflow}. Integrating Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) into SDN controllers enables autonomous selection of mitigation policies balancing security containment against network quality-of-service (QoS) degradation~\cite{b_yungaicela, b_drl_sdn}. Yungaicela-Naula et al.~\cite{b_yungaicela, b_drl_sdn} demonstrated the feasibility of DRL for autonomous DDoS defense in SDN/NFV environments. \section{Federated Edge-Cloud System Architecture}
\subsection{Architectural Overview}
As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig_arch}, the platform operates across four coordinated tiers:
(i) \textbf{Tier 1 (Edge Gateways)}: Distributed campus taps vectorize ingress flow records and execute local INT8 TCN-GRU sequence scoring;
(ii) \textbf{Tier 2 (Event Bus \& FL Coordinator)}: High-throughput Redis Streams buffer security events while the FedAvg coordinator aggregates client model weights;
(iii) \textbf{Tier 3 (Cloud Risk Engine \& DRL Control)}: Enriched telemetry is evaluated by an identity-context risk resolver and Deep Q-Network (DQN) policy agent;
(iv) \textbf{Tier 4 (SDN Actuation)}: Programmatic OpenFlow flow\_mods enforce Zero-Trust containment across the campus data plane.

\subsection{Edge Quantized Sequence Detection}
Campus flow telemetry is collected as rolling temporal windows $\mathbf{X}_t = [\mathbf{x}_{t-L+1}, \dots, \mathbf{x}_t] \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times D}$, where $L=10$ time steps and each vector $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$ contains $D=10$ non-DPI statistical flow features (packet count, byte count, flow duration, packet arrival rate, byte rate, mean packet size, active flow time, IP protocol, source port, and destination port). The edge model combines dilated 1D convolutions (dilations $d \in \{1, 2\}$, kernel $k=3$) to extract hierarchical multi-scale temporal receptive fields, followed by a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU, hidden dimension $H=32$) and linear classification projection:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_t = \text{GRU}(\text{TCN}(\mathbf{X}_t)), \quad \hat{y}_t = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_c \mathbf{h}_t + \mathbf{b}_c)
\end{equation}
To minimize edge gateway resource consumption, we apply PyTorch dynamic post-training quantization (\texttt{torch.quantization.quantize\_dynamic}) to the GRU and linear classifier layers, converting weights to signed 8-bit integers (INT8) while executing convolutional front-end layers in FP32~\cite{b_pytorch_quant}. This compresses the model memory footprint to $0.027$~MB ($27.5$~KB, a $38.2\%$ reduction from the $0.044$~MB FP32 model) while executing unbatched inference in $4.555$~ms.

\subsection{Collaborative Federated Averaging (FedAvg)}
Distributed campus nodes participate in decentralized model training without raw telemetry centralization. In each global communication round $t$, the central coordinator selects all $K=5$ active campus gateways, dispatches current global weights $\mathbf{w}_t$, and each edge node $k$ performs $E=1$ local training epochs over its dataset $\mathcal{D}_k$ minimizing binary cross-entropy loss:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{w}_{t+1}^k = \mathbf{w}_t - \eta \nabla \mathcal{L}_k(\mathbf{w}_t; \mathcal{D}_k)
\end{equation}
The cloud coordinator computes the synchronized parameter aggregation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{w}_{t+1} = \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{|\mathcal{D}_k|}{|\mathcal{D}|} \mathbf{w}_{t+1}^k
\end{equation}

\subsection{Asynchronous Redis Streams Bus \& Context Fusion}
Alert events $\mathcal{E}_i = \langle \text{timestamp}, \text{client\_id}, \text{flow\_tuple}, \hat{y}_t \rangle$ are published to an asynchronous Redis Streams bus (\texttt{security:telemetry:stream})~\cite{b_redis_stream} whenever the predicted anomaly probability satisfies $\hat{y}_t \ge \tau$, where $\tau = 0.50$ is calibrated on the training validation split. The Cloud Risk Resolver queries spatial-temporal user identity registries (mapping IP/MAC addresses to academic roles: Faculty, Student, IoT Sensor, Guest) and evaluates a contextual composite risk metric $R_t \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
R_t = w_1 \cdot \hat{y}_t + w_2 \cdot (1 - \text{Trust}(\text{Role})) + w_3 \cdot \text{Crit}(\text{VLAN})
\end{equation}
where weights satisfy $\sum w_i = 1.0$, calibrated to $w_1 = 0.50$ (model anomaly confidence), $w_2 = 0.30$ (untrusted identity penalty: $\text{Trust}(\text{Faculty}) = 0.9$, $\text{Trust}(\text{Student}) = 0.6$, $\text{Trust}(\text{Guest}) = 0.2$), and $w_3 = 0.20$ (VLAN asset sensitivity: $\text{Crit}(\text{Admin/DC}) = 1.0$, $\text{Crit}(\text{Academic}) = 0.6$, $\text{Crit}(\text{Dorm}) = 0.2$).

\subsection{Deep Reinforcement Learning (DQN) for Closed-Loop SDN Control}
We formulate autonomous SDN threat mitigation as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) $\langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{R}, \gamma \rangle$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{State Space} $\mathcal{S}$: $\mathbf{s}_t = [\hat{y}_t, \lambda_{\text{flow}}, R_t, c_{\text{alert}}, \text{node\_id}]$, capturing anomaly confidence, flow arrival rate, composite risk score, alert frequency, and client origin.
    \item \textbf{Action Space} $\mathcal{A}$: Discrete containment playbooks $\mathcal{A} = \{a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3\}$ (4 actions). In the Open vSwitch (OVS) testbed, $a_0$ performs monitoring; $a_1$ installs OpenFlow meter bands ($10\text{~Mbps}$); $a_2$ applies VLAN quarantine port tagging (mapping to identity-aware dynamic VLAN reassignment in production~\cite{b_zero_trust}); and $a_3$ installs OpenFlow hard-drop rules (mapping to edge BGP FlowSpec drops).
    \item \textbf{Transition Dynamics} $\mathcal{P}$: The environment state transition $\mathbf{s}_{t+1} \sim \mathcal{P}(\cdot \mid \mathbf{s}_t, a_t)$ directly reflects closed-loop network actuation: action $a_0$ allows active attacks to escalate subsequent link load; $a_1$ throttles malicious surges; $a_2$ isolates the client port, routing subsequent flows away from production servers; and $a_3$ installs flow drop rules, immediately relieving link congestion.
    \item \textbf{Reward Function} $\mathcal{R}$: Balances threat mitigation against operational disruption:
    \begin{equation}
    r(s_t, a_t) = \alpha \cdot \mathbb{I}_{\text{threat}}(a_t) - \beta \cdot \text{Cost}(a_t) - \lambda_{\text{fp}} \cdot \mathbb{I}_{\text{fp}}(a_t)
    \end{equation}
    where $\alpha = 10.0$, $\lambda_{\text{fp}} = 15.0$ penalizes false positive isolation of benign traffic ($\mathbb{I}_{\text{fp}}=1$), and $\beta = 1.0$ scales the operational disruption cost mapping: $\text{Cost}(a_0) = 0.0$ (Monitor), $\text{Cost}(a_1) = 0.5$ (Rate-Limit), $\text{Cost}(a_2) = 1.2$ (Quarantine VLAN), and $\text{Cost}(a_3) = 2.0$ (Flow Drop).
\end{itemize}

The DQN agent~\cite{b_dqn_nature} approximates action-value functions $Q(s, a; \theta)$ using an experience replay buffer $\mathcal{D}$ (capacity $10{,}000$) and a target network $Q(s, a; \theta^-)$ updated every $100$ steps with discount factor $\gamma = 0.95$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}} \left[ \left( r + \gamma \max_{a'} Q(s', a'; \theta^-) - Q(s, a; \theta) \right)^2 \right]
\end{equation}
The optimal policy selects $a^* = \arg\max_{a \in \mathcal{A}} Q(s_t, a; \theta)$, which triggers programmatic REST API calls to the Ryu SDN controller to install OpenFlow \texttt{flow\_mod} rules.

\subsection{Benchmark Datasets, Alignment \& Preprocessing Protocol}
We evaluate our architecture on two established intrusion datasets:
(i) \textbf{InSDN}~\cite{b_insdn}: A dedicated SDN intrusion benchmark featuring contemporary attacks (DDoS, DoS, Probe, brute-force attacks, Web Attack, Botnet, and U2R);
(ii) \textbf{CSE-CIC-IDS2018}~\cite{b_cicids2018}: Large-scale multi-vector enterprise network telemetry.

Both datasets are mapped to a unified 10-dimensional statistical flow feature schema ($D=10$: packet count, byte count, flow duration, packet arrival rate, byte rate, mean packet size, active flow time, IP protocol, source port, and destination port). To replicate real-world multi-campus statistical heterogeneity, dataset records from InSDN are partitioned across $K=5$ client edge nodes using a Dirichlet distribution $\text{Dir}(\alpha)$ with $\alpha = 0.5$ across multi-class traffic distributions. For federated training, each client $k$ trains locally on its partition, while evaluation partitions retain severe non-IID attack-class skew across campus client nodes. Crucially, to prevent test-set data leakage in zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation, the feature normalization scaler (\texttt{StandardScaler}) is fitted exclusively on the InSDN training partition; the learned scaling parameters $(\mu_{\text{train}}, \sigma_{\text{train}})$ are then directly applied to standardize the unobserved CSE-CIC-IDS2018 evaluation streams without any retraining or parameter fine-tuning.

\section{Discussion, Limitations \& Threats to Validity}
\textbf{WAN Network Jitter}: While local inference latency is deterministic ($4.555\text{~ms}$), federated weight synchronization over public WAN links is subject to network jitter. However, because FedAvg runs out-of-band at periodic intervals, communication delays do not affect real-time anomaly detection.

\textbf{Adversarial Poisoning}: Standard FedAvg remains vulnerable to model poisoning from compromised edge nodes. Future extensions will incorporate trimmed-mean aggregation and local Differential Privacy ($\epsilon, \delta$) guarantees~\cite{b_diff_privacy} to safeguard gradient updates.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented a Federated Edge-Cloud Resilience Architecture engineered for distributed campus network security. By synthesizing PyTorch dynamically INT8-quantized TCN-GRU sequence detectors, privacy-aware FedAvg aggregation, Redis Streams event distribution, and closed-loop Deep Q-Network SDN control, the platform achieves high anomaly detection fidelity ($F1 = 0.8582$, $ROC\text{-}AUC = 0.9729$ under uniform partitioning and $0.8097$ under Dirichlet-skewed non-IID evaluation distributions), sub-$15$ms automated threat mitigation ($12.34 \pm 0.55\text{~ms}$), and minimal edge resource overhead ($0.027\text{~MB}$ model size, $48.5\text{~MB}$ runtime RAM footprint, and $13.8\%$ CPU utilization under $10{,}000\text{~events/sec}$ throughput).
